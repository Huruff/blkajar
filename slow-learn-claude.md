You're describing a real risk, though it isn't true for everyone. People who use AI well often learn faster than before. The problem is mostly with how people use it.

**Why skills fade**

- **Skipping the struggle.** `Skills grow when you get stuck`, debug, and `fix things yourself`. `If AI removes every hard moment`, the `learning goes with it`.
- **Output without understanding.** Code that runs feels like code you understand, but `if you can't explain` it, `you can't fix` it `when it breaks`.
- **Weak fundamentals.** Without basics like data structures, how the web works, and how to read errors, you can't judge whether AI's answer is good or bad.
- **Presenting is a separate skill.** AI can make slides in seconds, `but delivery, storytelling, and answering questions live only improve with practice in front of people`. Slides you didn't think through are hard to present.

**How to prevent or reverse it**

1. **Do it yourself first, then use AI.** Try the problem for 15 to 30 minutes, then ask AI for help.
2. **Ask AI to teach, not just do.** For example: "`Explain this line by line`," or "Give me a hint, not the answer."
3. **Never ship code you can't explain.** After AI writes something, `explain it back in your own words`. `If you can't, ask until you can`.
4. **Practice without AI regularly.** Build a small project or solve a few exercises with AI turned off, like going to the gym.
5. **Learn the fundamentals.** A short course on programming basics pays off because it lets you review AI's work.
6. **For presenting:** let AI help with structure, but write the key message yourself. Then rehearse out loud, record yourself, and present to real people. Ask AI to play a tough audience and quiz you with hard questions.

A simple rule: `use AI` to `go faster` at things you `already understand`, and `go slower` on purpose with things you want `to learn`.
